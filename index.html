03

Online Retail Customer Analytics & Segmentation

+Capstone: RFM scoring and clustering with a marketing action per segment

The problem

  • Which customers and products drive revenue, when and where do people buy, and can customers be grouped into segments that marketing can act on?

Result

~0Kinvoice lines analysed (UCI Online Retail)
0customer segments (K-Means)
~0silhouette score

Approach

Cleaning→EDA→RFM scores→Log + scaling→K-Means→Segment actions

Key decisions

  • Guest invoices without a CustomerID were kept for sales analysis but excluded from RFM and clustering.
  • Returns were kept in a separate table (net revenue = sales + returns) and non-product codes moved to an adjustments table; outliers were kept because wholesale buyers are real customers.
  • RFM scored 1 to 5 with pd.qcut; k = 4 chosen using silhouette, the elbow method and the need for a manageable number of groups.
  • Cross-checked with Hierarchical clustering (silhouette 0.33). DBSCAN flagged 80 extreme customers (1.9%) and was used for outlier detection rather than segmentation.

Findings

  • Revenue is concentrated in a small share of products and customers; the UK is the main market and sales peak in the last months of the year.
  • Sales happen on weekdays, mostly around midday, with signs of bulk / wholesale buying.
  • Each of the 4 segments gets a different action, such as rewarding the best customers and sending win-back offers to valuable customers who stopped buying.

Limitations I documented

  • Only about a year of data; no cost or profit data; guest customers excluded; moderate silhouette means segments overlap a little.
